Emergency Towing in Brisbane: 10 Things to Keep in Mind

Getting stranded on the side of the road is stressful enough, but when it happens in a busy metropolitan area like Brisbane, knowing exactly what to do can save you a mountain of stress, time, and money. Whether you have had a sudden mechanical failure on the Gateway Motorway or a collision during peak hour on the Riverside Expressway, being prepared is your best defense.

When the unexpected happens, having a trusted, professional team like OG TOWING in your contacts is a lifesaver. Operating 24/7 with a fast, 30-minute response time across Brisbane and Redlands, they take the sting out of roadside emergencies.

To help you handle your next roadside incident smoothly, here are 10 critical things to keep in mind about emergency towing in Brisbane.

1. Prioritise Your Immediate Safety First

Before you even pick up the phone to call for assistance, secure your immediate environment.

  1. Pull over to the far-left shoulder or an emergency breakdown bay if possible.
  2. Keep your hazard lights flashing.
  3. If it is safe to remain in the vehicle with your seatbelt on, do so. If you must exit, do so from the side away from active traffic.

2. You Have the Right to Choose Your Towing Company

In Queensland, you are not obligated to use the first tow truck that arrives at an accident scene. Under state laws, you have the absolute right to choose who transports your vehicle. Professional providers like OG Towing respect this right and work directly with you—not high-pressure spotters—to ensure your car is recovered safely.

3. Understand Queensland’s Strict Towing Regulations

Queensland has strict legal frameworks governing towing. The Tow Truck Act 2023 and Tow Truck Regulation 2024 manage crash-related towing, towing from private property, and licensing requirements. Any legitimate emergency towing service operating in Brisbane must be accredited and use authorised tow trucks.

4. Know the Maximum Regulated Towing Fees

To protect consumers, the Queensland Government sets caps on towing fees within regulated areas for crash towing and private property parking removals.

  1. Crash Towing: The regulated maximum fee is $447.90 for the first 50km, plus $8.90 per kilometre over that distance. This fee includes up to 1 hour of working time at the scene and clearing debris.
  2. For general breakdowns, look for transparent providers who quote upfront. For example, local light-vehicle towing from OG Towing starts from an affordable, flat-rate pricing structure.

To review the current regulated fees and consumer rights, check the official guidelines on the Queensland Department of Transport and Main Roads (TMR) Tow Truck Scheme.

5. Clarify Your Destination

Don’t let your vehicle be towed to an unknown holding yard where storage fees accumulate daily. Always instruct the driver where you want the vehicle taken—whether that is your home address, your preferred local mechanic, or a specific holding yard. If you are unsure, reliable operators will help you find a safe storage solution.

6. Take Photos of Your Vehicle’s Condition

Before the tow truck driver hooks up your car, take quick photos of the vehicle from multiple angles. This simple step creates a clear record of your vehicle's physical condition prior to transport, which is highly useful for insurance claims.

7. Prepare Your Vehicle for Towing

If you are involved in an accident, your comprehensive car insurance policy typically covers the cost of emergency towing. Make sure to choose a towing company that works directly with major Australian insurers. Tow Truck near Capalaba provides seamless insurance towing, handling the direct billing and paperwork on your behalf so you can focus on recovering.

8. Beware of Hidden Call-Out Fees

Some predatory towing operators quote a low base rate but stack on hidden fees, such as "difficult access fees", weekend surcharges, or administration costs. Always ask: "Does this quote include everything to get my car to the destination?" True professionals will always offer direct, transparent pricing with no hidden surprises.

9. Know What Happens if You're Towed by Council

If you park illegally in a clearway, a "no stopping" zone, or across a driveway, the Brisbane City Council has the legal authority to tow and impound your vehicle.

If you believe your car has been seized by the city, you can contact the Brisbane City Council Towed and Seized Vehicles Division to find your vehicle’s reference number and arrange its release.
