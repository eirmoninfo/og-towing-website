Can You Move an Unregistered Car in Queensland? Permits, Rules & Towing Explained (2026)

The car has been sitting in the driveway for eight months, the rego lapsed somewhere back in autumn, and now you need it gone — to a mechanic, to a buyer in Logan, or off the property entirely. The problem is that the moment those wheels touch a public road in Queensland, you are breaking the law unless you have done one of two things first.

This guide walks through both options: getting an unregistered vehicle permit, or skipping the paperwork completely by having the car towed. If you already know the vehicle is not safe to drive, jump straight to arranging car towing across Brisbane and Redlands — it is almost always the cheaper and faster route.

Why Unregistered Vehicles Are a Bigger Problem in Brisbane in 2026

Queensland drivers underestimate this one constantly, and the enforcement environment has tightened:

  • Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR) cameras are fitted to Queensland Police patrol cars and mounted at fixed roadside sites, scanning plates against the registration database in real time.
  • Speed and red-light cameras across South East Queensland also check registration status — you can be detected without ever being pulled over.
  • Driving unregistered means you have no Compulsory Third Party (CTP) insurance. If you cause injury in a crash, you can be personally pursued for the damages.
  • Registration must be transferred within 14 days of a sale in Queensland, which is why so many recently-bought cars end up in this grey zone.
  • Brisbane’s motorway network — the Gateway, the M1, the Ipswich Motorway — is heavily camera-covered, so “it’s only a short drive” is rarely as low-risk as it sounds.

The short version: a ten-minute drive to the mechanic is not worth the fine, and it is definitely not worth the uninsured liability.

What Is an Unregistered Vehicle Permit (UVP)?

An unregistered vehicle permit is a temporary authorisation from the Department of Transport and Main Roads (TMR) that lets you legally move an unregistered car, trailer or caravan on Queensland roads for a specific journey. It is not a substitute for registration — it covers one trip, for one purpose.

According to the Queensland Government’s unregistered vehicle permit guidelines, a permit is only available for vehicles that are eligible for registration, and the vehicle must be in a safe condition to drive. The driver has to carry the permit for the whole journey. If you are towing an unregistered trailer behind a registered car, the permit still needs to be with the driver of the towing vehicle.

You also need Class 22 CTP insurance for the journey, and the permit must be bought in the state or territory where the trip starts — so a Brisbane-to-Gold-Coast move is a Queensland permit, not a New South Wales one.

1. When You Actually Need a Permit (and When You Don’t)

Not every unregistered movement requires paperwork. In most cases you can drive an unregistered vehicle directly to a safety certificate (roadworthy) inspection or to a TMR customer service centre for registration without a permit — provided you carry a CTP insurance certificate and the old plates have been removed. Anything outside those exempt purposes generally needs a permit.

You will typically need a UVP when you are:

  • Moving a car from a private sale to your home across Brisbane
  • Relocating a caravan or trailer that has been off the road for years
  • Driving a project or restoration vehicle to a workshop that is not a roadworthy inspection
  • Transporting a vehicle interstate after a sale

2. How to Apply for a UVP in Queensland

There are two paths, and neither is instant:

  • Online. Apply through the TMR service portal. You need a Queensland Customer Reference Number (CRN) — usually your driver licence number.
  • In person. Complete form F3523 at a transport and motoring customer service centre.

Either way you pay the unregistered permit fee and arrange Class 22 CTP cover before the permit becomes usable. Then you drive the approved route, on the approved day, with the permit physically in the car.

3. The Rule Most Brisbane Drivers Miss: Tow Trucks Don’t Need a Permit

This is the part that saves people the most time and money.

A tow truck is permitted to tow an unregistered vehicle under section 11 of the Transport Operations (Road Use Management—Vehicle Registration) Regulation 2021 — a point TMR sets out plainly in its authorised tow truck requirements.

In practical terms: if the vehicle goes on the back of a truck, you do not need a UVP, you do not need Class 22 CTP for the trip, you do not need a CRN, and you do not need to wait for an application to be processed. You call, the truck arrives, the car moves. That is why local car towing is the default choice for most unregistered vehicle moves around Capalaba, Cleveland, Carindale and the wider Redlands.

4. When Towing Is the Only Legal Option

A permit requires the vehicle to be in a safe, roadworthy-standard condition. That immediately rules out a permit if the car:

  • Has failed brakes, steering or suspension
  • Has been in a collision and is structurally damaged
  • Has flat or perished tyres from sitting for months
  • Has no working lights or indicators
  • Will not start at all

In every one of those cases, a permit application will be refused and towing is your only lawful way to move the vehicle.

5. Selling or Scrapping Instead of Moving

If the reason you want the car gone is that it is not worth registering again, moving it becomes someone else’s job. Cash for cars in Brisbane services buy unregistered, damaged and non-running vehicles and include removal in the deal — no permit, no CTP, no transport cost on your side.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming “it’s only down the road.” ANPR does not care about distance.
  • Leaving the old plates on. Plates must come off an unregistered vehicle — driving with them attached is a separate offence.
  • Buying a permit for an unroadworthy car. It will be refused, and you have lost the application time.
  • Forgetting the 14-day transfer window after a private sale, which is how most cars end up unregistered in the first place.
  • Booking an unaccredited operator. Tow trucks that attend incidents, police seizures or private-property removals in regulated areas must hold TMR accreditation.
  • Skipping CTP because the trip is short. Uninsured liability for injury is unlimited.
